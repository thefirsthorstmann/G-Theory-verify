## 1 · Introduction

Tonal function has three standard accounts. Rameau derived harmony from the
fundamental bass; Riemann organized it into three functions; the perception
laboratory measures the stability hierarchies directly. Each describes the
offices of the degrees, and none derives why exactly these degrees carry
exactly these offices; §12 states the relation of this paper to each.

The companion volumes derived the form of gravitation, its coupling, and the
vacuum's structure from the arithmetic of the reptend 1/7 = 0.142857…, realized on
the figure of nine seats of this series, with the tones seated at exact ratios. Those
volumes used tonal function throughout — the lift at Fa, the rest at La, the
leading tone's axis — and derived the functions nowhere. This paper closes that
account. It claims six things. The existence of a unique tonic is decided by a
symmetry count — a theorem. The location of function is the semitone joints — a
theorem. The relation of dominant to subdominant is a reflection — a theorem.
The authentic cadence's resolution applies exactly one unit of each generator —
a theorem on the lattice the series fixed long before this paper. Each function
holds a named physical office in the companion volumes — a table with its
translation grammar stated. And the measured perceptual hierarchy is consonant
with the arithmetic — an observation cited as the standard result it is.
The dimensional boundary of the Units volume stands untouched throughout: none of
this yields a magnitude.

## 3 · The functions, defined

Tonal function is the observed fact that the degrees of the scale are not
interchangeable: each carries a characteristic office, stable across melodies,
keys, centuries and listeners. The traditional names are Tonic at Do, Dominant at Sol, Subdominant at Fa, the relative minor's root at La,
the leading tone at Si.
The standard accounts describe these offices. The claim of this paper is that the
offices are seated in the arithmetic, as follows, with the derivations in §§4–6:

**Definition.** On the ring of twelve positions, negation — the reflection through
the root — fixes exactly two positions: 0 and 6. **The Tonic is the occupied
fixed point.** The scale sits on 0 and omits 6: **the tritone position is the
empty fixed point**, the one address negation preserves and the scale declines.
**Dominant function is the activation of the empty fixed point**: the chord of
the fifth degree with its seventh contains the interval that spans the ring's
diameter through position 6 without seating it. **Subdominant function is the
Dominant's mirror image**, by §5. **La is the reversal's seat**: the one degree
whose descending address is the fifth read backwards. **Si is the joint that
leads**, by §4: the upper of the two semitone seams, one step below closure.

These are definitions with content, because each names an arithmetic property a
degree either has or lacks, and the theorems below show the properties select
exactly the traditional offices — the derivation of the dictionary, not a
relabeling of it.

## 4 · The unique root, and the two joints

**Theorem 1 (the unique root condition).** A pitch class set supports a unique
tonic only if its transpositional stabilizer is trivial — only if no nonzero
shift of the ring maps the set onto itself. If a shift t fixes the set, every
candidate root has an indistinguishable alternative at distance t, and no interior
evidence can decide between them.

The count, run on the standing scales: the diatonic set and the pentatonic set
are fixed by no shift — a unique root is nameable in both. The whole tone set is
fixed by six shifts, the octatonic by four, the full chromatic by all twelve.
These are the scales of limited transposition, and on their own arithmetic the
root is six-, four- and twelve-fold ambiguous: **tonal function in them is not
difficult but impossible**, forbidden at the level of the set before any note
sounds.

The corroboration is three centuries of practice, and it is cited as
corroboration rather than as the theorem. Whole tone writing floats and is
prized for floating; the octatonic is the standard resource for suspended
tonality; the undifferentiated chromatic is atonality's home. And Messiaen, who
catalogued these sets, named them for exactly the property the theorem counts —
the limitation of their transpositions. Practice found by ear what the stabilizer
counts by shift.

The condition is necessary and not sufficient: a set may pass the count and
still lie inert. What directs a passing set is the joint structure:

**Theorem 2 (the two joints).** The diatonic step word is 2 2 1 2 2 2 1: exactly
two semitone seams, and they fall at Mi–Fa and at Si–Do. The whole tone word is
2 2 2 2 2 2: no seam at all.

The joints are where function lives. The seam at Si–Do is the leading tone's:
one step below closure, the place the scale leans hardest, and the upper voice
of every authentic cadence. The seam at Mi–Fa is the lift's: the place the
companion volume on gravity located the miss of one sign that sources the pull.
A scale with no seams has nowhere to lean from and nowhere to lean to — nothing
leads, nothing lifts — which is the whole tone floating of Theorem 1 restated
mechanically. The two theorems together give the paper's first plain sentence:
**a scale has function when it can name its root and has joints to move
through, and the diatonic seven is the standing set that does both.**

## 5 · The mirror: the relative behaviour of the functions

**Theorem 3 (the reflection).** The reflection of the ring through the axis at
the midpoint between Do and Sol — the axis that exchanges the two — carries the
major set onto its parallel natural minor. The same reflection carries the
dominant seventh — Sol, Si, Re, Fa — onto the set Do, Le, Fa, Re: the
subdominant minor chord with added sixth.

The consequences are the relative behaviours the musician already navigates.
**Dominant and subdominant are one object in two orientations**: the chord that
presses toward the tonic from the fifth above and the chord that settles onto it
from the fifth below are mirror images, exactly. This is why the authentic and
plagal cadences feel like two directions of one move. It is also why the
traditional account needed exactly three functions and no fourth: tonic, and
one function read twice.

The same reflection runs through the harmonic series itself. Take the recipe
"the first three prime partials of Do" and run it upward: partials 2, 3 and 5,
reduced by octaves, give Do–Sol–Mi — the major triad, the overtone chord, the
dominant side. Run the identical recipe downward — divisors 2, 3 and 5,
raised by octaves — and it gives Do–Fa–Le: the minor triad on the subdominant side.
One recipe, two directions; the major and minor sonorities are the mirror pair
of §5 heard as ratio, and each undertone address is exactly the octave over its
overtone partner: 4/3 = 2 ÷ 3/2, 8/5 = 2 ÷ 5/4. What is claimed here is the
arithmetic and only the arithmetic: whether any physical system sounds an
undertone series is a separate and contested question this paper does not
enter. The ratios mirror; that is the claim, and it is checked in a line.

Two further relative facts complete the section, both exact on the lattice on 24.
**The fifth below is the fourth above**: 48 × 2/3 = 32 = 24 × 4/3 — descending
Sol lands on Fa's own number, so the subdominant is the dominant heard from the
octave above, one seat with two approaches. **And La is the reversal's seat**:
48 × 5/6 = 40 = 24 × 5/3, the degree at 240° whose descending address is 2/3,
the fifth read backwards. This is why the relative minor rests there. It is
also why the companion volume on gravitation seats the same 2/3 as the
vacuum's fraction of the cosmological budget, in its §14.

## 6 · The cadence uses one three and one two

The strongest progression in tonal music is the dominant seventh resolving to
the tonic, and its unrest has a single source: the chord contains Si against
Fa — the interval that spans the ring's diameter through the empty fixed point.
On the lattice the interval is 45 against 32, the ratio 45/32: **the one address
the figure never seats**, lying between Fa's 4/3 and Sol's 3/2. The chord does
not sit on the empty fixed point — no tone does — it stretches across it, and
the stretch is the tension.

**Theorem 4 (the resolution's ledger).** The tritone resolves by contrary
motion, both voices moving the same just semitone 16/15: Si rises 45 → 48 and
Fa falls 32 → 30. In lattice units the voices move +3 and −2 — one unit of
each generator, in opposite directions — and the opened interval is
45/32 × (16/15)² = 8/5, the Mi–Do frame of the tonic chord.

Read what the theorem says. The two whole numbers out of which every ratio in
this series is built are 2 and 3. The cadence — the move Western harmony
selected, out of everything available, as the sound of arrival — moves one
voice up by exactly a 3 and one voice down by exactly a 2. The pull the
musician feels at the dominant seventh is the arithmetic's own pair, enacted in
contrary motion through the one address the figure declines to occupy. The
companion volume derived a force from a one-signed miss at Fa; this is the same
grammar at the scale of a phrase. **Tension is an unseated address, resolution
is the application of the generators, and arrival is the occupied fixed point.**

One layer deeper, stated as the structural parallel it is. The series' wheels
carry two families of moduli: the all nines integers, which close a period, and
their companions the integers of the form 10^h + 1, which negate one. Return
and crossing are the two faces of every even wheel. The authentic cadence is a
crossing: it passes through the diameter, the half turn, the negation — and the
plagal cadence, the mirror's cadence, arrives without crossing. That the two
cadences of common practice align with the two moduli families of the wheel is
recorded here as a reading of the same arithmetic. The arithmetic itself —
the factorization of every even wheel into closure times negation — is exact
and machine checked in the companion suite.

## 8 · The physical offices, made explicit

The companion volumes did not use tonal vocabulary as ornament; the functions

process that can be checked rather than a resemblance that can be admired.

**The grammar, in four steps.** Given a ratio: read it operationally — multiply
and divide separately, count the operations in the two generators, and find the
residue, which is where the work lives. Seat it — the descending ratio times the

## 9 · The perceptual characteristics, noted

Two bodies of observation sit beside the derivation, and their standing is
marked plainly: they are recorded as consonant with it, not claimed as derived
from it.

**The measured hierarchy.** The perception laboratory has quantified tonal
function for four decades with the probe-tone method of Krumhansl and
colleagues: play a context that establishes a key, sound a probe
tone, and ask listeners to rate its stability. The obtained profiles are the
standard result of the field, replicated across training levels and cultures
with variations that are themselves studied. They order the degrees: the
tonic most stable, then the fifth, then the third, then the remaining scale
tones, then the chromatic residue. That ordering is the arithmetic's: the
occupied fixed point first, then the overtone triad, then the seated degrees,
then the unseated addresses. The consonance is noted at exactly that strength.
What would make it more than consonance — a perceptual experiment whose design
is derived from the seat table and whose outcome the arithmetic constrains in
advance — is proposed in §11 as this paper's own test.

**The wider pattern, held as a signpost.** These series are mathematics felt in
sound before they are mathematics seen on paper — that is the fact of the
psychology, however it is finally explained. But the same structures are not
confined to sound. The companion volumes read the electromagnetic and
gravitational forms out of first order directivity — the monopole and dipole
whose sum is the cardioid — and the figure's own geometry is the spiral of
seats this paper's table walks. Where a material pattern in nature displays
spiral or cardioid structure, the series' characteristics are available to the
eye as they are to the ear. The definiteness of the perceptual response in
both channels is recorded here as a signpost. It suggests an understanding of
perception's relation to these forms deeper than this paper derives, and it is
left standing as exactly that.

## 10 · Interlude: for the musician

*The companion volumes carry interludes teaching tonality to the physicist.
This one runs the other way.*

A coupling constant is physics' name for how hard something pulls, written as a
pure number. The gravitational coupling of the electron — how hard two
electrons gravitate, compared with everything else they do — is a number so
small it takes forty-five digits to write. The standard account can only
measure it and file it. The companion volume found it is not an arbitrary
number: it is a five circulating on a wheel one hundred fifty-one places long —
a single tone, held on a very long string. You already own the skill this
series runs on. When you hear a dominant seventh and know, before it moves,
where it must go, you are reading unseated addresses and counting what closure requires — the same reading this series does on the constants of nature. The
claim of these papers, said for the player: the universe's numbers behave like
voices, because voices and numbers are both built from two and three, and
nothing built from two and three gets to be arbitrary.

One theorist deserves naming in this interlude. George Russell's *Lydian
Chromatic Concept of Tonal Organization* (1953) organized harmony around
what he called tonal gravity: the pull of every tone toward a tonic. He
located its source in the ladder of fifths, the same generator this paper
counts, and the companion volume on gravitation — which reads the
gravitational field as a tonal center — meets his phrase from the other
side, seven decades on. The difference is structural and instructive.
Russell generates his parent scale one-sidedly, by consecutive fifths
upward from the tonic; this yields the Lydian set and omits the
subdominant. The present construction seats the diatonic set two-sidedly,
one generator step below the tonic and five above, and the mirror of
Theorem 3 — dominant and subdominant as one object in two orientations —
lives exactly in the step his stack omits. On this account his instinct
was arithmetic fact, and the two-sided placement is what makes it
provable.

## 11 · What would refute this

1. **Theorem 1.** A repertoire establishing functional harmony — cadential
   arrival distinguishable by listeners at better than chance — inside a scale
   of limited transposition, without tones from outside it, refutes
   the unique root condition. None exists; the theorem forbids one.
2. **Theorem 2.** A functional idiom whose characteristic motions avoid the
   semitone joints — whose cadences move only by whole tone — refutes the
   joint location. The standing repertoires do the opposite.
3. **Theorem 3 and the triads.** The reflection identities are a line of
   arithmetic each; an error in any is decisive and checkable by hand.
4. **Theorem 4.** The ledger is exact on the lattice: if the just cadence's
   voices do not move 16/15 in contrary motion applying +3 and −2, the
   resolution reading fails. The check is four integers.
5. **The offices.** The table of §8 falls wherever a companion volume's
   mechanism falls; it has no independent life, and claims none.
6. **The proposed test.** Construct probe contexts in scales whose stabilizers
   differ — diatonic, pentatonic, whole tone, octatonic — and measure root
   inference. The arithmetic requires: unique root scales yield convergent
   root judgements; scales of limited transposition yield judgements split among
   the stabilizer's orbit, six ways and four ways respectively. A measured
   unique root, convergent across listeners, inside the whole tone set would
   refute Theorem 1 in the laboratory as surely as a repertoire would in the
   literature.

## 12 · Relation to the standard accounts

Rameau derived harmony from the fundamental bass and the corps sonore. Riemann
gave the three functions and proposed the undertone dualism his critics took a
century contesting. The negative harmony tradition rediscovered the mirror as a
compositional resource, and the perception laboratory measured the hierarchies. To
each, this paper adds the same two things. A derivation: the offices follow
from a symmetry count, a step word, a reflection and a four integer ledger, on
seats the companion volumes fixed for other reasons entirely. And a
prohibition: no scale of limited transposition supports a unique tonic —
a forbidding sentence none of the standard accounts states, because none of
them counts stabilizers. Where Riemann's dualism claimed a sounding undertone
series and was refutable there, the mirror here is arithmetic and is refutable
only as arithmetic. Where the perceptual profiles are measurements in search of
a mechanism, the mechanism proposed is the seat table, and §11.6 states the
experiment that would test it.
