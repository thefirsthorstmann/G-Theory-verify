## 1 · What is G-Theory?

**What is it?** A research programme that derives the dimensionless structure of
physics — coupling constants, mass ratios, the shape of physical law — from the
arithmetic of the first incommensurable pair, 2 and 3, realized as the mode
structure of a vibrating string. The construction carries no adjustable
parameters: nothing is tuned to match observation, so every derived number
stands exposed rather than fitted.

**What does it not claim?** No dimensionful magnitude — no length, mass or time
in metres, kilograms or seconds — is derived. A magnitude is a dimensionless
structure multiplied by one measured reference, and the corpus proves that
boundary as a theorem and works inside it. Where the single borrowed reference
enters, it is declared.

**How is it held to account?** Three ways. Every claim is graded for exactly
what it is. Every exact claim re-derives by machine, in a public suite of 1,161
assertions, before it is asserted. And every paper states, on its own face, the
findings that would refute it. This document assembles those conditions in one
place.

## 2 · How to read this sheet

Every record of the corpus is taken in turn, grouped in four parts: the
construction and the results a measurement decides; the results settled by
argument; the mathematical papers; and the framing documents. Each entry gives
the problem the record addresses, in the terms the field uses for it; the
position the record takes; and the conditions under which it fails. Where a
measurement decides the question, the entry carries a table naming the claim,
what refutes it, the instrument expected to decide it, and the date it comes
due. Where the question is settled by argument — by arithmetic, a
counterexample, or a structural fact — that is said plainly, so the boundary
between the two kinds of condition is as visible as the conditions themselves.

## 4 · Gravity on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the standing problem of gravitation's place in fundamental
physics: why it resists the quantum description every other interaction accepts,

gravitation is the reference level of the register — the tonal centre against
which every other quantity is read, unsounded itself — and that its coupling
follows from the arithmetic with no fitted parameter. The inverse-square law is
obtained by counting rather than assumed. Newton's constant is derived and put on record in advance of the metrology that
will decide it, at a value 106 parts per million below the CODATA centre. That
value lies well inside the 551-ppm spread of the input determinations, and is

## 6 · Yang–Mills: Existence on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the Clay Institute's Yang–Mills existence and mass gap
problem. The position, argued by theorem, is that the object the axioms demand requires
an actual infinity of available structure in every bounded region. The mode
ledger of a region is an octree, and the Wightman and Osterwalder–Schrader
axioms make every level of it available without bound. Three standard bounds of
physics then limit what any physical system can occupy of that structure —
Planck's occupancy relation, the Bekenstein bound as derived inside quantum
field theory by Casini, and the Margolus–Levitin theorem. Each is logarithmic in
the resources, so a proton's entire rest energy cannot obtain a single quantum

object is therefore a supertask, and what remains open is a property of the
notation rather than of the gauge field. The mass gap itself is addressed on the
field's terms in three registers: a theorem of the lattice formulation at strong
coupling, a measurement of the scalar glueball near 1.7 GeV, and a magnitude
fixed by dimensional transmutation. No theorem of continuum mathematics is
disputed anywhere in the paper, and asymptotic freedom is accepted at full

## 7 · QED on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses renormalization: the procedure that makes quantum

had no agreed interpretation since it was introduced. Its integrals over

exhaustive check over five candidates. What the paper does not compute — the
magnitude of the running, and the anomalous magnetic moment, both of which are

## 9 · Tonal Function on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses tonal function: the observation that the degrees of a
scale carry characteristic offices — tonic, dominant, subdominant and the rest
— stable across repertoires and listeners. The standard accounts of Rameau,
Riemann and the probe-tone laboratory describe the offices precisely but take
them as primitive.
The position is that the offices follow from the exact ratios at which the
companion volumes seat the tones, in four theorems. A set supports a unique
tonic only if no transposition maps it onto itself, which admits the diatonic
and pentatonic sets and prohibits the whole-tone, octatonic and chromatic ones.
Function is located at the two semitone seams of the diatonic step word, exactly
where functional harmony moves. The reflection exchanging tonic and dominant
carries the major set onto its parallel minor, so dominant and subdominant are
one object read in two orientations. The dominant seventh's tritone spans the
ring's one unseated address and resolves by applying one unit of each generator.

## 10 · Navier–Stokes: A Solution on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the Clay Institute's Navier–Stokes regularity problem: it
is not known whether smooth solutions of the incompressible equations can
develop a singularity in finite time. The position is that finite-time
singularity formation in a turbulent cascade is a supertask, requiring the
completion of infinitely many distinguishable physical operations in finite
time. The proof is elementary and complete. The turnover schedule of the Kolmogorov cascade converges — the times permit
the supertask, which is why the continuum problem is genuinely open on its own
terms. The operation demand through cascade depth N grows exponentially,
however, and the Margolus–Levitin theorem caps what a fluid of finite energy
can execute. The distinction the paper draws
is between the mathematical question, which it leaves untouched, and the
physical one, which it closes.

**What would refute it.** An exhibited bounded physical system executing more
distinguishable operations than the Margolus–Levitin bound allows; or a
demonstration that the cascade's operation demand does not grow as claimed,
which is a finite arithmetic check the public suite performs.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21197045*

## 11 · The Fine-Structure Constant on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the derivation of the fine-structure constant, a target
with a long record of failure. The failures share an anatomy: a formula tuned to
the measured decimal, announced after the measurement, and adjusted when the
measurement moved. The position is that the constant separates into three
strata, and that a different kind of claim is made for each. The integer 137 is
treated as arithmetic — it is the unique prime whose reciprocal has base-ten
period eight and seventeen cyclic families, which follows from an exhaustive
check over the five prime divisors of 10⁸ − 1. The block .036 is read from the
structure. The digits beyond it are declined, and the paper says why: the field's
own best determinations disagree with each other beyond that depth, so no
account should claim what the instruments have not settled.

**What would refute it.** A structural condition, motivated as independently as
the one given, that selects a different prime; or the removal of the assignment
of the count to the electromagnetic office. The uniqueness check itself is
finite and can be repeated in a few lines.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21211051*

## 12 · Motion on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the foundations of classical kinematics, which assumes
rather than derives its own starting points. Inertia is posited as a law, and
the quadratic dependence of kinetic energy on velocity is taken from
experiment. Angular momentum is conserved by appeal to a symmetry, and orbital
motion is fixed by a variational principle adopted from outside. The position is that four
of these follow from a single discrete structure. Inertia is obtained as a
theorem of parity — the two generators admit no common closure, so the substrate
has no fixed point and no rest state for a body to occupy. The speed limit
follows from the wheel's definition, in which the advance and the tick are one
event.

**What would refute it.** A common value 2^p = 3^q with positive exponents would
restore a rest state and refute the first theorem; the parity of integers
forbids it. A rider advancing more than one rung in one tick refutes the second.
A motion cost scaling other than quadratically refutes the third in a line of
arithmetic. Each remaining condition is settled in a few lines of algebra that
the public suite executes on every revision.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22119337*

## 13 · Units on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the relationship between dimensionless structure and
dimensioned magnitude, and what that relationship implies for the present system
of units. The position is that a physical magnitude is a dimensionless structure
multiplied by one measured reference, and that the reference is chosen rather
than derived. Three results follow. A rescaling theorem: under any change of
unit every dimensionless ratio is invariant, so no experimental agreement or
disagreement can be created or removed by redefinition, and any claim that a
rescaling improves a comparison is false. A register theorem, fixing the length
of a repeating block in any base. And the dimensional boundary itself — that no
dimensionless construction yields a dimensionful magnitude — which is the
theorem the whole corpus is disciplined by, and the reason the papers above
claim ratios and decline lengths.

**What would refute it.** An exhibited construction that produces a dimensionful
magnitude from dimensionless structure alone, with no borrowed reference. The
boundary is a theorem about theories, and is tested by attempted counterexample
rather than by instrument. The invitation stands open to every reader.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22119361*

## 14 · Visible Light on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the structure of the colour wheel, which no standard
account derives from anything smaller. Six hues stand in a circle, three of
them additive primaries and three subtractive, each secondary sitting between
the two primaries that make it, complementary pairs opposite. The band of visible light
spans about one octave, and vision has no octave equivalence, so the spectrum is
a segment rather than a circle; perception closes it through the purples, which
have no wavelength of their own. The position is that the closed wheel is therefore a mixing fact. Its entire
structure — the count of six, the two triads, the interleaving, the complement
rule — is computed from the six rotations of a single repeating decimal, and
the mixing arithmetic is exact at both poles.

**What would refute it.** A colour wheel in use whose complements did not sum to
white, or whose primary triads did not interleave, would leave the arithmetic
with nothing to name. No such wheel is in use. The claim is structural, and it
is settled by the wheel itself rather than by an instrument.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22119205*

## 15 · π on Discrete Terms

The paper argues that the sciences do not, in practice, obey the identification

routes wearing a value. 2π is one closure of a cycle, 4π is a point's flux into
three-space, and 8π in the Einstein field equations is doubled flux rather than

## 17 · The Enneagram Is a Theorem
