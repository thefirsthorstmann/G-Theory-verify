## 1 · Introduction: a brief survey of falsifiability in modern physics

Falsifiability is fundamental to science. A theory whose statements admit no
way of being confirmed or refuted can hardly be considered science, because
science proceeds by testing hypotheses against experiment and evidence. The
classical formalization of this criterion is due to Popper: a theory is
scientific to the degree that it forbids, exposing itself to observations that
could refute it [1]. The formalization has known limitations, examined at
length in the philosophy of science. Its core has not been displaced: a claim
that no observation could fail is not doing empirical work.

For most of the twentieth century, fundamental physics met this requirement as
a matter of course. General relativity was confirmed at the 1919 eclipse by a
prediction that could have failed. The Standard Model predicted the W and Z
bosons, the top quark and the Higgs boson decades before the machines that
found them, and each prediction could have been wrong. The authority physics
now holds was established in this way, by forbidding outcomes and passing the
tests.

The present situation at the foundations is different, and the field itself
has begun to say so. The leading candidate frameworks include the string
landscape, with its immense space of possible vacua, eternal inflation and its
multiverse, and anthropic selection among the possibilities. Between them they
have exposed no measurement, accessible to any foreseeable instrument, by which
they could fail. In 2014, in Nature, the cosmologists George Ellis and Joseph
Silk warned that accommodating such frameworks moves the field away from
defining scientific knowledge empirically [2]. They asked the community to
defend the integrity of physics. A school of response argues that theoretical
virtues can stand in for measurement, under the name of non-empirical
confirmation [3]. The dispute is genuine, it is conducted by serious people,
and it is unresolved.

Physics is the inquiry into the nature of reality, and the world depends on its
results in two ways: materially, in every technology built on its findings, and
in its picture of what is real. An inquiry carrying that weight has honesty as
its first obligation, before elegance, ambition or institutional success.
Falsifiability is where that obligation becomes operational, as the discipline
by which an inquiry remains correctable. A claim protected from every possible
failure cannot carry the authority of a tested one. A field that admits such
claims at its foundations places the reliability of its account of the world
at risk.

Hence this document, and its place at the front of the series: the conditions
on which this corpus can fail, gathered in one place.

## 3 · How to read this document

Every record of the corpus is taken in turn, grouped in four parts: the
construction and the results a measurement decides; the results settled by
argument; the mathematical papers; and the framing documents. Each entry gives
the problem the record addresses, in the terms the field uses for it; the
position the record takes; and the conditions under which it fails. Where a
measurement decides the question, the entry carries a table naming the claim,
what refutes it, the instrument expected to decide it, and the date it comes
due. Where the question is settled by argument — by arithmetic, a
counterexample, or a structural fact — that is said plainly, so the boundary
between the two kinds of condition is as visible as the conditions themselves.

**What follows a refutation is stated in both halves.** A condition that is met
is recorded as a refutation and is not reinterpreted. The program then uses the
miss to find why the construction failed, and revises its positions and techniques on that
evidence. Every revision is dated: a revised statement enters as a new one
under its own date, answerable to later measurements, and the failed one stays
on the record. Where an entry reports a standing in tension, the next version of this
document states how it resolved.

Each entry follows its record as the record now stands, and every measured
value is read again at its source for this version. Changes of substance
from version 3 are marked † and noted where they occur.

## 12 · Navier–Stokes: A Solution on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the Clay Institute's Navier–Stokes regularity problem: it
is not known whether smooth solutions of the incompressible equations can
develop a singularity in finite time. The position is that finite-time
singularity formation in a turbulent cascade is a supertask, requiring the
completion of infinitely many distinguishable physical operations in finite
time. The proof is elementary and complete. The turnover schedule of the
Kolmogorov cascade converges, so the times permit the supertask, which is why
the continuum problem is open on its own terms. The operation demand through
cascade depth N grows exponentially, however, and the Margolus–Levitin theorem
caps what a fluid of finite energy can execute. The paper distinguishes the
mathematical question, which it leaves where it stands, from the physical one,
which it closes.

**A bounded physical system executing more distinguishable operations than the
Margolus–Levitin bound allows would refute the paper.** So would a
demonstration that the cascade's operation demand does not grow as claimed,
which is a finite arithmetic check the public suite performs.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21197044*

## 13 · The Fine-Structure Constant on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the derivation of the fine-structure constant, a target
with a long record of failure. The failures share an anatomy: a formula tuned to
the measured decimal, announced after the measurement, and adjusted when the
measurement moved. The position is that the constant separates into three
strata, and that a different kind of statement is made about each. The integer
137 is arithmetic: it is the unique prime whose reciprocal has base-ten period
eight and seventeen cyclic families, by an exhaustive check over the five prime
divisors of 10⁸ − 1. In the first decimal block the numerator 36 is
over-determined by three constructions that share no steps, and its placement
at the third decimal place follows through the nucleon masses and remains open
from the arithmetic alone. The rest value 137.036 stands 6.0 parts per billion
above the CODATA 2022 value, and every modern determination lies below it. The
digits beyond the block are left to measurement: the best determinations agree
through 137.035999 and disagree at the next digit. Twenty-eight times the
settled block 0.035999 is 1.007972, within 1.3 parts per million of the mean of
the proton and neutron masses.

**A measured α⁻¹ at or above 137.036 refutes the reading of the block.**† An
arithmetic error in any pinned exhibit refutes the statement it carries, and a
larger census showing the paper's three registers to be common would weaken
the case. A structural condition, motivated as independently as the one given,
that selects a different prime would remove the assignment of the count to the
electromagnetic office and leave the arithmetic standing. The uniqueness check
is finite and can be repeated in a few lines.

## 14 · Motion on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the foundations of classical kinematics, which assumes
rather than derives its own starting points. Inertia is posited as a law, and
the quadratic dependence of kinetic energy on velocity is taken from
experiment. Angular momentum is conserved by appeal to a symmetry, and orbital
motion is fixed by a variational principle adopted from outside. The position is that four
of these follow from a single discrete structure. Inertia is obtained as a
theorem of parity — the two generators admit no common closure, so the substrate
has no fixed point and no rest state for a body to occupy. The speed limit is
a counting bound of one place per tick, with admissible velocities forming a
bounded ladder of rationals. The quadratic form of kinetic energy follows from
the structure's energy form applied to per-tick increments, and conservation
of angular momentum from the append-only winding counter. The orbit equation
follows from these four together with one assumed principle of stationarity,
named where it is used.

**Each theorem is refuted by a named counterexample.** A common value
2^p = 3^q with positive exponents would restore a rest state and refute the
first; the parity of integers forbids it. A rider advancing more than one rung
in one tick refutes the second. An energy of motion scaling other than
quadratically refutes the third in a line of arithmetic. A tick count not
recovered from windings and phase refutes the fourth. A realized configuration
that is not stationary at fixed winding refutes the assumed principle, and the
orbit equation with it.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22119336*

## 15 · Units on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the relationship between dimensionless structure and
dimensioned magnitude, and what that relationship implies for the present system
of units. The position is that a physical magnitude is a dimensionless structure
multiplied by one measured reference, and whether that reference must be
supplied or in one case can be derived is the question the record explores. The
dimensional boundary is its first theorem: no construction
from dimensionless structure alone determines a dimensionful magnitude. The
second fixes the number of external references at exactly one, because the
rescalings that leave every dimensionless quantity fixed form a one-parameter
group. Under any change of unit every dimensionless ratio is invariant, so no
agreement or disagreement between theory and experiment can be created or
removed by redefinition. A constant that enters the defining expression and
the reporting unit at the same power cancels, and the reading is silent about
it at any precision. The 2019 revision of the SI fixed four constants and set
four quantities free to be measured. A proposal labelled as metrological
rescales the metre by 0.0692 percent, giving the speed of light the numeral
3 × 10⁸. The single reference belongs on a quantity defined at the
zero-excitation limit, such as the proton charge radius or the atomic mass
unit. The record's hypothesis: the structure itself names the object that
carries its one reference, the atomic mass unit, with the base 1.00800 read from
the first operation on the reptend of one seventh, under two stated conventions.
The magnitude in any outside unit remains the one measurement.

**Seven prohibitions are stated, each with the condition under which it fails.**
The first is the boundary itself, refuted by an exhibited construction that
produces a dimensionful magnitude from dimensionless structure alone, with no
borrowed reference. It is a theorem about theories and is tested by attempted
counterexample rather than by instrument. The others forbid a theory with zero or with two independent references, and a
disagreement removable by a choice of unit. They also forbid a complete
positional reading at a length off the period, and a constraint on a constant
through a channel in which it cancels. The seventh forbids a second
self-identifying unit. The hypothesis is
refuted by a measured nucleon rest value, in atomic mass units, outside the
registered conditions of the proton and neutron record (§11).

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22119360*

## 16 · Visible Light on Discrete Terms

This paper addresses the structure of the colour wheel, which no standard
account derives from anything smaller. Six hues stand in a circle, three of
them additive primaries and three subtractive, each secondary sitting between
the two primaries that make it, complementary pairs opposite. The band of visible light
spans about one octave, and vision has no octave equivalence, so the spectrum is
a segment rather than a circle; perception closes it through the purples, which
have no wavelength of their own. The position is that the closed wheel is
therefore a mixing fact. Its entire structure — the count of six, the two
triads, the interleaving, the complement rule — is computed from the six
rotations of the decimal expansion of one seventh, and the mixing arithmetic
is exact at both poles. Multiplication by ten arranges the six fractions k/7
in a circle on which opposite fractions are complements, and alternate
positions split them into the quadratic residues and the non-residues. The
identification of the rotations with the hues is an interpretation, with six
namings left free. A constraint on mixing or hue spacing that measurement
could refuse remains the open problem.

**A colour wheel whose complements did not sum to white, or whose primary
triads did not interleave, would refute the reading.** It would leave the
arithmetic with nothing to name, and no such wheel is in use. The claim is
structural, and it is settled by the wheel itself rather than by an
instrument.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22119204*

## 17 · π on Discrete Terms

The paper argues that the sciences do not, in practice, obey the
identification analysis makes between two constructions that agree in the
limit, and that π is the clearest case. The working coefficients of physics
are construction routes presented as values: 2π is one closure of a cycle, 4π
is a point's flux into three-space, and 8π in the Einstein field equations is
doubled flux rather than eight of anything. π arises from the counting of
binary outcomes with no circle drawn, and no finite set of finite-precision
measurements distinguishes it from a rational. The midpoint of every
Archimedean bracket lies above π, with the ratio of excess to deficit tending
to exactly 2. The two smallest registers, 22/7 from above and 201/64 from
below, have the midpoint 2815/896, a rest value 47 parts per million above π.

**The conjecture that the circle's constant at zero excitation is its rest
value is refuted for counts.**† The counting of atoms in a sphere of silicon-28 is the one measurement in
which the circle constant does not cancel. It agrees with the Kibble-balance
route to four to seven parts in 10⁸, and with the rest value in place of π
the count would move by 47 parts per million. The
conjecture stands as a statement about an operation not yet named, and naming
that operation is the paper's open problem. The paper is refuted by a
physically realized completed infinite process, a bracket midpoint below π,
or a demonstration that route-distinct coefficients do the same explanatory
work.

## 18 · The Seventh — A Constant Hidden in Plain Sight

The decimal period of one-seventh, 142857, is a familiar curiosity of
elementary arithmetic, and nearly everything known about it is old. The paper
credits the classical results first — the cyclic property, the multiplication
behaviour, Midy's theorem of 1836 — because its own claims lie elsewhere. The position is that the reptend is a mathematical constant in its own right,
exact in the sense in which π and e are exact. It is a lossless six-digit
object whose halves sum to nine and whose label set coincides with the first
six harmonics of a vibrating string. Its two canonical orders, positional and
multiplicative, carry the structure this corpus builds on. Four exact and machine-pinned exhibits carry the case. They are the label set; the collapse of the six sevenths onto the root, the
fourth and the major sixth; the transform that exchanges the two canonical
orders by one conserved operation; and the fold across Midy's axis. On them the paper
defines the reptend cycle of seven as the harmonic constant and proposes it
for the tables of fundamental constants, beside the fine-structure constant.
An arithmetic error in a pinned exhibit refutes the content, and a prior
statement of the collapse, the transform or the fold would retire the paper's
statement of novelty.

## 19 · The Enneagram Is a Theorem

The enneagram is a figure of nine points on a circle, with a six-point path
1→4→2→8→5→7 and a triangle 3–6–9. It has circulated for a century through
esoteric literature, its construction from the period of 1/7 expounded
obliquely and never derived.† The definitive edition is in two parts. Part I
treats the figure as a combinatorial object of modular arithmetic. Its centre
is the line theorem: the figure of a prime p in base b consists of exactly
(p − 1)/ord_p(b) closed strokes, one exactly when b is a primitive root
modulo p. The count of cyclic classes is classical, from the theory of decimal
periods that Gauss completed, and the paper's statement is its reading as the
strokes of the drawn figure. For the pair (7, 10) the one stroke passes through
six distinct points, which form the doubling orbit of 1 modulo nine, and the
moving path never enters the triangle. The sixty closed forms on the six
points fall into twelve classes under the figure's twelve symmetries and are
the orderings of a one-loop diagram with six legs. The alternating forms are
the color-flow basis of three quarks and three antiquarks. Z/9 admits exactly
one proper nontrivial quotient, whose kernel is the triangle.

## 20 · Zero and Infinity on Discrete Terms

The divergences and paradoxes of modern physics sit at zero or at infinity:
the ultraviolet catastrophe, the self-energy divergences, the singularity
theorems, renormalization, instantaneous collapse in measurement and Zeno's
paradoxes. The paper states as one position, argued across its companion
records, that zero and infinity are boundary markers of a bounded quantized
system, names for edges and not places where anything stands. Three results
carry the case. A completed infinity cannot be performed, since finite-time
singularity formation is a supertask excluded for any finite-energy system by
the Margolus–Levitin bound. A completed limit cannot be certified by any
finite set of finite-precision measurements. The one empirical test of an
actual infinity, classical equipartition in a finite cavity, ended when Planck
replaced it with a count. For zero the position is symmetric: every realized
quantity has a least count, and zero is reached by notation and never by
process. Analysis as mathematics is unchanged. The position is refuted by a
physically realized completed infinite process, or by a realized exact zero
that acts.

*The paper: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21209363*

## 21 · The Vanishing Point on Discrete Terms

Within the real numbers 0.999… = 1 is a theorem: the decimal denotes the limit
of its partial sums, and that limit is 1. The proof rests on two assumptions
that no finite procedure carries out, a completed infinite tail of nines and
the completeness of the real numbers. The paper examines the equality
operationally, under the program's hypothesis that physical reality is
discrete and harmonic, with no completed infinity. Four exact results concern
the partial sums. Every partial sum, read as the integer of its digits, has
digital root 9, and reaching 1 requires supplying the missing unit, which is
the rounding itself. The ratio of 1 to the n-th partial sum is 10ⁿ/(10ⁿ − 1),
superparticular for every n. At every finite n the equation 10x = 9 + x misses
by exactly 9 · 10⁻ⁿ, and each deep partial sum differs from 1 by exactly 10⁻ⁿ.
What physical process could supply the missing unit remains open. The paper is refuted by a partial sum whose digital root is not 9, a ratio
that is not superparticular, or a partial sum satisfying 10x = 9 + x. A
finite test separating the limit from every deep partial sum refutes it as
well.
